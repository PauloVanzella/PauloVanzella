## More than a `whoami`

I’m interested in the point where different fields stop looking separate.

Code is one of the ways I explore that. So are design, languages, chess, visual thinking and research. I like following a question far enough that it forces me to learn something I did not expect to learn.

I’m less interested in fitting everything into one title than in understanding **how things connect** — how systems are structured, how an interface feels, how a strategy emerges, or how a language changes the way an idea can be expressed.

**For me, making is a form of thinking.**

| Curiosity | Systems | Craft | Iteration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Follow questions before titles. | Look for relationships, patterns and structure. | Care about how things work — and how they feel. | Learn by making, breaking and rebuilding. |

## Chess, code & other systems

Chess interests me for many of the same reasons software does: a small set of rules can create enormous complexity.

That curiosity became **[Chess-AI](https://github.com/PauloVanzella/Chess-AI)** — a Python experiment around chess and machine learning, trained from millions of board positions.

At a very different level of abstraction, **[Do-Re-Mi-Fa-Song](https://github.com/PauloVanzella/Do-Re-Mi-Fa-Song)** uses Assembly on a PIC16F628A to simulate a piano with LEDs and play a song.

The distance between those projects is part of the point. I enjoy moving between layers — from low-level instructions to interfaces, from logic to aesthetics, from a technical problem to the human experience around it.

## Principles I try to keep

**Observe.** Notice what is easy to overlook.  
**Question.** Defaults are useful, not sacred.  
**Connect.** Distant ideas often become more useful together.  
**Make.** Turn thought into something concrete.  
**Stay curious.** Understanding is more interesting than appearing certain.
